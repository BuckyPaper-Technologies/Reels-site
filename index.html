7 reels that make clients come to us

One creator, seven stories where someone else’s result makes the viewer ask “how?”, and every call to action is an invitation to apply. Tone

Demand-first: they come to us

The new spine for all seven reels: Hook (curiosity, contrarian or status, 0–3 s) → Someone else’s result (3–10 s) → The question “how?” → Short reveal → The tell (word of mouth, selection or a reveal) → Invitation to apply, comment or request access. Series sign-off: “Built in Chennai. Ask for it by name.”

Show the after, not the pitch

Every reel opens inside someone else’s result: a calm office, a parent’s phone, a wedding compliment. We never open on our logo, our price or a problem we beg to solve.

Let a character ask the question

A neighbour, a parent, an aunty or another owner asks “who did this?” on camera. The viewer hears their own question answered before we sell anything.

Withhold a little

Reveal the how in 8–10 seconds of screen, not a feature tour. Curiosity brings people to the profile and the website.

Selective, not desperate

We take on a limited number of companies, schools, stores and boutiques each month, and we choose partner boutiques. Every call to action invites the viewer to apply, comment or request access.

Qualify the people who come

Meta lead forms use the higher-intent setting and 3 qualifying questions, so the leads that arrive are ones we want to talk to.

Never discount on camera

No “offer”, “hurry” or “DM for price”. Ecom Suite’s ₹10,000 appears as a reveal that surprises the neighbour, never as a bargain banner.

Keep it honest. Scarcity and selection lines (“limited each month”, “not every boutique makes it”, “Chennai first”) only go live if they are true; write the real cap in each lead form. Staged scenes with “customers” or “owners” carry a small “Dramatisation” label unless they are real clients who gave written consent. Misleading claims can get ads rejected and damage the trust this tone depends on.
Cross-check

What current Reels and Meta guidance says, and what we changed

Checked on 12 November 2026 against recent industry guides. Specs and policies change often, so confirm in Meta Ads Manager and the Meta Business Help Center before launch.

Hook in the first 3 seconds

Meta’s hook rate (3-second views divided by impressions) is a key quality signal for Reels, and most viewers decide in the first 3 seconds.

What changed in the scripts: Every reel has 3 hook variants (curiosity, contrarian, status). Shoot all three openings with the same body and test them. Source

Two lengths per reel

Organic Instagram Reels between 45 and 60 seconds showed the highest engagement in one large dataset, while ads work best when tight.

What changed in the scripts: Keep the 35–45 s version for organic posting and cut a 15–20 s version for paid placements using the cut-down plan on each reel. Source

Sound on, but captions always

About 40% of viewers watch silently, so every reel must make sense with captions only.

What changed in the scripts: All lines carry burned-in English subtitles and bold on-screen text; Tanglish stays in the audio. Source

9:16 with safe zones

Meta recommends 9:16 at 1440 × 2560 for Reels ads and says to keep text and logos away from the top, bottom and sides, where the interface overlays the frame.

What changed in the scripts: Frame the creator and text in the centre of the screen; use Meta’s Safe zone guardrail in Ads Manager before publishing. Source

Short ad copy

For Reels ads Meta recommends roughly 40 characters of primary text and 55 for the headline; feed placements show about 125 characters before “See more”.

What changed in the scripts: Each ad pack below has a primary text of 40 characters or fewer and a headline of 55 or fewer. Source

Keywords over hashtag piles

Instagram’s recommendations now lean on keywords in captions; about 5 hashtags is the current common advice.

What changed in the scripts: Captions are written as keyword-rich sentences with 5 hashtags, not long hashtag lists. Source

Carousels as a companion

Carousels are reported as Instagram’s highest-reach format, so a post that works as a carousel adds reach beside the reel.

What changed in the scripts: Each reel has a 4-slide carousel copy so the same idea can run as a static post or Meta carousel ad. Source

Disclose AI and creators

Meta labels AI-generated or heavily AI-edited ads, and reports say branded creator content used in paid campaigns must run as Partnership Ads. Check the latest rules in Meta’s Business Help Center before launch.

What changed in the scripts: Fabele reels show AI try-on of realistic people, so enable AI disclosure. Run creator footage as Partnership Ads if the creator’s handle is used. Source

Launch plan

How to run these as organic posts and Meta ads

Week 1: test hooks.
  • Shoot the 3 hook openings for every reel.
  • Post the full organic cut with the best-looking hook.
  • Run the 15–20 s cut as 3 ads, one per hook, with identical body, text and button.
Week 2: keep winners.
  • Compare 3-second view rate first, then cost per qualified application.
  • Pause weak hooks; make 2 new hooks from the winner’s style.
  • Run a Tamil-led and an English-led version for the same audience.
Placements and format.
  • Upload 9:16 video at 1440 × 2560 (minimum 1080 × 1920).
  • Turn on the Safe zone guardrail and keep key text in the centre.
  • Use original audio or Meta’s licensed sound library for ads.
Landing pages.
  • Send each ad to the product URL shown on the reel, with an “Apply” or “Request access” form, not a price list.
  • Repeat the offer, inclusions and any “extra cost” line on the page (especially Ecom Suite).
  • Add a short privacy line for Fabele measurements.
